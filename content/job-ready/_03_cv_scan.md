{{% note %}}
**0:10 · The scan runs by itself, then five clicks (Space or ↓).**
- On entry the amber dot is the recruiter's eyes. Seven seconds. It lands on the photo, the objective, the skill bars, and never finds a link. Stamp: Next.
- Click 1: photo and personal info go. Age, religion, and status don't help you. Many international companies prefer CVs without them, to avoid bias.
- Click 2: drop the "objective". Write the job you want and your stack in one line.
- Click 3: skill bars mean nothing. 80% of Java? Write the skills as words. That's also what the CV software searches for.
- Click 4: link every project. GitHub, live demo, LinkedIn.
- Click 5: one page, simple Word file. Stamp: Interview.
{{% /note %}}

{{% note %}}
**0:12 · LinkedIn · Five clicks (Space or ↓).**
- Click 1, **headline**: not "Student at University". Say what you do and your stack.
- Click 2, **about**: two or three plain lines. What you build, what you want next.
- Click 3, **skills**: the same words as your CV. Recruiters search these.
- Click 4, **open to**: turn it on. Internships, junior roles.
- Click 5, **connections**: engineers, alumni, recruiters, your classmates. Then add experience, projects, activities, and ask for recommendations.

{{% note %}}
**0:14 · Internships.**
- One to three real internships. Mix them: a startup, a mid-size company, a big one. Each one teaches you a different job.
- My Atypon internship turned into my first full-time job. That's common. Interns are the easiest people to hire.
- No internship yet? Volunteer, freelance for a small shop, or join an open source project. It all counts.
- Bridge →: "So you got past the seven seconds. Next filter: can you actually do it?"
